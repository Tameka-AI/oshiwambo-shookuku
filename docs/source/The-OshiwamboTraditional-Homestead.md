# The OshiwamboTraditional Homestead

The Traditional Homestead

Oshiwambo Traditional Homestead    (Source: Williams, 1991:185)

1. 1 The Entrances

Each homestead is surrounded by a fence called ongandjo. The ongandjo was construceted with large mopane tree poles sharpened into points at the top and was used as a defence against enemies and wild animals.

The homestead consists of two entrances: The main entrance (eelo/onhu) which faces the eastern direction to symbolize prosperity and good luck. Every commoner has to enter the house through the onhu.  The okanto (a small back entrance) is situated on the western side of the homestead. The okanto is used in case of emergency, for example the entire family can use it in case of attack.  Another small entrance that leads from the main kitchen (elombe) to the granaries section is called etambo. When a king pays a visit, he comes in the homestead through this entrance because it is believed that if he passes through the entrance that is used by the commoners, he will die or become permanently disabled.

1.2 The Meeting Place (oshinyanga/oshoto/olupale)

A homestead consists of at least three meeting places. The main oshoto is situated directly opposite the wife’s sleeping room. The fire is kept burning at the main oshoto to protect the family from evil spirits and bad omen and also to strengthen the ties between tha family and the ancestors. The herdsmen are also served here with food when they are going to the cattlepost. The bride or the bride groom is also blessed here before he/she enters into marriage. There is also a meeting place where the soldiers going to the war are served with food. This is known as oshoto shiita. The other oshoto is called oshoto shamutyakemo (the meeting place of the one who is not in) which is located near the sleeping hut of the head of the household. Only special and respected guests are invited to this oshoto. If the husband does not like the visitor, then the visitor is informed that he is not in. Storytelling and games playing take place at the main oshoto.

1.3 The Kitchen (epata/elugo)

It is where food is prepared in the homestead.  There are three cooking stands called omasiga. The omasiga are made out of loam soil. If one of the children dies then one of the omasiga is replaced with the esiga made from the old and slightly damaged clay pots or loam soil. The main food cooked at elugo is oshithima. The important stick for cooking is called oluko. It is eaten with beef, spinach soup, mopane worms, etc. The oluko is given to the youngest child to feed on it.The important hut found in the kitchen is elimba (pantry) in the oluyo luufila  ( a pot in which flour is kept at all times ) is kept and other cooking utensils are also kept in elimba. It is believed that when oluyo is empty, the boys who are at the cattle post will fall victim to hunger and bad luck. A dried spinach is kept under the flour to prevent accidents from occurring to the family members who are way from home.  The senior wife and second wife are buried in the epata.

1.4 The Birthing Hut  (osakalwa)

When the mother is about to give birth, she moves in Osakalwa. She is accompanied by the midwife who attends to her labour. The midwife cleans the baby with red ochre mixed with fat and ties the umbilical cord. The baby is seated on the ongalo (sifting basket. The ongalo symbolize good luck. The midwife gives a child a name reflecting the circumstances surrounding the birth.

1.5The Senior Wife’ s Bedroom (ondjugo)

The ondjugo for munyalombe (senior wife) is located directly opposite oshoto in the homestead. Other men cannot not go near her room or else they will be fined by the husband. It remains open at all times so that the husband can see what is happening inside.. The Oshimbale shomondjugo in which valuable things are kept, is kept in the ondjugo. The was lit in the room of the wife. It was made of mopane poles and the pole were plastered together with the clay

1.6 Head of the Household Bedroom.

Traditionally the husband has to sleep separately from hi s wives. Hi omutala was located next to the cattle so that he protects the cattle against the raids. All weapons such as bow and arrows , axe, spear etc. were kept in the omutala of the husband. No fire was lit in the room of the husband. The poles of the room are not plastered.

1.7 The Girls Sleeping Quarters

The bedrooms are situated between the senior ‘ s wife room and the room of the head of the household so that the girls cannot sneak out at night. Young children sleep in the room of their mother where they are taught norms and values.

1.8 The Boys Sleeping Quarters

The boys sleeping quarters are located next to the cattle kraal so that they protect the cattle against raids. The oshoto for the boys is located to the west of the main entrance. The visitors were received at this oshoto by children or junior wives.

1.9 Granaries (omashisha)

These are mahangu storage containers which are constructed by men. They are made of mopane barks.When a man is making a granary, he cannot leave the granary unfinished to attend the funeral. He also does not make contact with family of the deceased until he has finished making the granary. No one is allowed to open the seal of a granary without the permission of the husband. The granary was only unsealed by munyalombe. It was the task of the second wife to distribute foodstuffs to the other kitchen. When the husband dies the granaries are inherited by his maternal relatives.

1.10 The Pounding Area (oshini)

The oshini is the area in which mahangu is pounded into flour.. Other food products were also refined and processed at oshini. A long pestle is used to pound mahangu into flour.

It is located along the main passage leading to oshoto. It consists of pounding blocks that are dug in into the ground. The floor is plastered with the clay mixed with sand. Traditionally when junior wives and girls are buried in the oshini.

1.11. The Cattle Kraal (oshigunda)

It is divided into sections for cows, bulls, goats, calves and sheep. An old man was buried in the section for bulls when dies. The boys were buried in the section for calves.

1.12 Ondundu yomalovu (hut for for beer and palm and marula wines.

It is located opposite the husbands sleeping hut.
